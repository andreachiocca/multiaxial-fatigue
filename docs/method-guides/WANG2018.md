# Wang–Shang–Sun–Chen weighted-plane criterion with mean shear stress (`WANG2018`)

> **Registry name:** `WANG2018` · **Catalogue entry:** [N17](../../METHODS.md#n17) ·
> **Source:** Wang, Shang, Sun & Chen (2018), <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tafmec.2017.10.007> ·
> **Implementation:** [`cp_methods/wang2018.py`](../../src/multifat/models/cp_methods/wang2018.py)

## Overview

- **Type and family:** critical plane, stress based. The critical plane is the weighted mean of the
  instantaneous maximum-shear planes over the cycle. On it, the shear and normal stresses are
  combined in von Mises form, each corrected by its own mean stress.
- **Idea:** a mean shear stress shortens the life, as a mean normal stress does. Its effect enters
  through $\lvert\tau_m\rvert$, so the sign of the mean shear does not matter. The sensitivities
  follow from the reversed torsion limit and the pulsating tension and torsion limits.
- **Proposed for:** high-cycle lives of 7075-T651 thin-walled tubes under tension–torsion. The
  paper covers constant amplitude (in and out of phase, with mean stresses) and variable
  amplitude.
- **Status:** constant-amplitude branch, tension–torsion. The variable-amplitude procedure is not
  implemented.

## Original formulation

**Stresses on a plane** (Eqs. 1–2). The tube carries the axial stress $\sigma(t)$ and the shear
stress $\tau(t)$. Let $\varphi$ be the angle between the normal of a plane perpendicular to the
surface and the axis. Then

```math
\sigma_\varphi = \frac{\sigma}{2} + \frac{\sigma}{2}\cos2\varphi + \tau\sin2\varphi ,\qquad
\tau_\varphi = \frac{\sigma}{2}\sin2\varphi - \tau\cos2\varphi .
```

**Weighted critical plane** (Eqs. 3–7). At each instant $t_k$ of one cycle:

```math
\tau_{\max}(t_k) = \sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma(t_k)}{2}\right)^2 + \tau(t_k)^2},\qquad
\varphi_{-45^\circ\text{–}45^\circ}(t_k) = \frac12\arctan\left[-\frac{\sigma(t_k)}{2\tau(t_k)}\right],
```

```math
W(t_k) = \frac{\tau_{\max}(t_k) - \min\tau_{\max}}{\max\tau_{\max} - \min\tau_{\max}},\qquad
\hat\varphi = \frac{\sum_k \lvert\varphi_{-45^\circ\text{–}45^\circ}(t_k)\rvert\,W(t_k)}{\sum_k W(t_k)} .
```

$\hat\varphi$ takes the sign of $\sum_k \varphi_{-45^\circ\text{–}45^\circ}(t_k)W(t_k)$.
The shear stresses on $\hat\varphi$ and on $\hat\varphi + 90^\circ$ have the same magnitude, so the
candidate with the larger damage is the critical plane $\varphi_c$. If $\tau_{\max}$ is constant
(e.g. $90^\circ$ out of phase with $\tau_a/\sigma_a = 0.5$, Eqs. 8–9), the plane of the largest
normal stress is critical.

**Damage parameter** (Eqs. 11, 15, 18). On the critical plane, with amplitudes $\tau_a$, $\sigma_a$
and means $\tau_m$, $\sigma_m$:

```math
\tau_{eq} = \sqrt{\left[\tau_a + k_\tau\lvert\tau_m\rvert\right]^2 + \frac13\left[\sigma_a + k_\sigma\sigma_m\right]^2},
\qquad
k_\tau = \frac{2\tau_{-1}}{\tau_0} - 1,\quad
k_\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{48\tau_{-1}^2}{\sigma_0^2} - \frac{12\tau_{-1}^2}{\tau_0^2}} - 1 .
```

$\tau_{-1}$ is the reversed torsion limit. $\sigma_0$ and $\tau_0$ are the pulsating (R = 0)
limits, written as maximum stresses. Eq. (17) requires $k_\tau > 0$ and $k_\sigma > 0$. When the
pulsating limits are not available, Eq. (19) estimates them:

```math
\sigma_0 = \sqrt2\,\sigma_{-1},\qquad \tau_0 = \frac{4\tau_{-1}}{2\sigma_{-1}/\sigma_0 + 1} .
```

**Life** (Section 4.2): $\tau_{eq}$ is entered on the fully reversed torsional S–N curve.

## Repository implementation

The two stresses are the axial normal stress $\sigma_{yy}$ (the repository's axis is $y$) and the
shear $\tau_{xy}$. One cycle is sampled at 3600 instants for Eqs. (3)–(7). On a plane, the
amplitude and the mean of one harmonic are exact: amplitude $\sqrt{s^2 + c^2}$ for sine and cosine
coefficients $s$ and $c$. The model returns $\tau_{eq}$ (MPa).

**Repository choices**

- *Scope.* Tension–torsion only, the paper's loading. A hoop or radial normal stress, or any other
  component, raises `LoadingScopeError` (maintainer decision).
- *Candidate planes.* $\hat\varphi$ and $\hat\varphi + 90^\circ$ are compared through
  $\tau_{eq}$. There is one S–N curve, so the larger $\tau_{eq}$ is the larger damage.
- *Constant $\tau_{\max}$.* The plane of the largest maximum normal stress
  $\max_t\sigma_\varphi(t)$ is critical; it is searched over 3600 planes. A zero signed sum
  (e.g. pure tension, where $\pm\hat\varphi$ are equivalent) gives $\hat\varphi \ge 0$.
- *Constants.* $\sigma_{-1}$ and $\tau_{-1}$ are the workbook `Sigm1` and `Taum1`. The paper takes
  them from its S–N curves at $N_0 = 10^6$, $2\cdot10^6$ or $10^7$. $\sigma_0$ and $\tau_0$ are
  `Sig0` and `Tau0`; when missing they come from Eq. (19), with $\tau_0$ computed from $\sigma_0$
  as given or as estimated. Constants that violate Eq. (17) raise `MaterialScopeError`.
- *Life.* The shared master curve $\tau_{eq} = A N_f^b$ is fitted on the R = −1 pure torsion
  failures (`design_loading = "torsion"`). On those tests $\tau_{eq} = \tau_a$, so this is the
  paper's torsional S–N curve.
- *Variable amplitude.* Wang–Brown counting and Miner's rule on the weighted plane (Eqs. 21–24)
  are not implemented.

## Calibration

1. **Original procedure.** $k_\tau$ and $k_\sigma$ come from $\tau_{-1}$, $\sigma_0$ and $\tau_0$
   (Eq. 15), or from $\sigma_{-1}$ and $\tau_{-1}$ through Eq. (19). The torsional S–N curve is
   fitted on the fully reversed torsion tests. Nothing is fitted on multiaxial tests.
2. **Repository.** The same. The master curve is fitted on the R = −1 pure torsion failures; a
   workbook without them skips the model (`DesignSetError`).

## Worked example

Constant-amplitude tests of Table 5 (7075-T651). The limits come from the Table 2 S–N curves at
$N_0 = 10^6$. The last column applies the Table 2 torsional curve $\tau = 688.85\,N^{-0.12233}$.

```python
import numpy as np

from multifat.models.cp_methods.wang2018 import wang2018_constants, wang2018_tau_eq
from multifat.models.registry import get_models

wang = get_models(["WANG2018"])[0]
# 7075-T651, Table 2 S-N curves at N_0 = 1e6 (Section 5.2)
f_m1, t_m1 = 888.90 * 1e6**-0.10042, 688.85 * 1e6**-0.12233
k_tau, k_sigma = wang2018_constants(f_m1, t_m1)
print(f"f_-1 = {f_m1:.1f}, t_-1 = {t_m1:.1f} MPa; k_tau = {k_tau:.4f}, k_sigma = {k_sigma:.4f}")


def tube(s_m, s_a, t_m, t_a, delta_deg):
    """Harmonic parts of sigma = s_m + s_a sin wt (axis y), tau = t_m + t_a sin(wt - delta)."""
    d = np.radians(delta_deg)
    S_mean, S_sin, S_cos = np.zeros((3, 3)), np.zeros((3, 3)), np.zeros((3, 3))
    S_mean[1, 1], S_sin[1, 1] = s_m, s_a
    S_mean[0, 1] = S_mean[1, 0] = t_m
    S_sin[0, 1] = S_sin[1, 0] = t_a * np.cos(d)
    S_cos[0, 1] = S_cos[1, 0] = -t_a * np.sin(d)
    return {"S_mean": S_mean, "S_sin": S_sin, "S_cos": S_cos}


params = {"Sigm1": f_m1, "Taum1": t_m1}
for delta in (0, 30, 90):  # Table 5, specimens 30, 38, 46
    h = tube(163.02, 144.57, 94.12, 83.47, delta)
    sigma = (h["S_mean"][1, 1], h["S_sin"][1, 1], h["S_cos"][1, 1])
    tau = (h["S_mean"][0, 1], h["S_sin"][0, 1], h["S_cos"][0, 1])
    _, phi_c = wang2018_tau_eq(sigma, tau, f_m1, t_m1)
    out = wang.evaluate_case(S0=None, S1=None, E0=None, E1=None, params=params, harmonics=h)
    tau_eq = out.values[""]
    N = (tau_eq / 688.85) ** (-1 / 0.12233)  # Table 2 torsional S-N curve
    print(f"delta = {delta:2d} deg: phi_c = {np.degrees(phi_c):6.1f} deg, "
          f"tau_eq = {tau_eq:5.1f} MPa, N = {N:8.0f}")  # fmt: skip
```

Output:

```text
f_-1 = 222.0, t_-1 = 127.1 MPa; k_tau = 0.2071, k_sigma = 0.8698
delta =  0 deg: phi_c =   69.6 deg, tau_eq = 159.3 MPa, N =   157636
delta = 30 deg: phi_c =  -20.4 deg, tau_eq = 160.0 MPa, N =   152280
delta = 90 deg: phi_c =  -20.1 deg, tau_eq = 160.4 MPa, N =   149120
```

With Eq. (19), $k_\tau = (\sqrt2 - 1)/2$ for every material (Eq. 20).

Out of phase, the plane normal lies about $20^\circ$ from the axis, so the crack trace is at about
$70^\circ$. This is the paper's estimate (Section 5.1); the measured traces were $60^\circ$ and
$65^\circ$. The experimental lives are 53 058–65 046 cycles (0°), 64 231–82 445 (30°) and
70 333–94 718 (90°). The predictions lie within a factor of 3, on the non-conservative side, as
in the paper's Figs. 11–12 at $N_0 = 10^6$.

## Comparison capabilities and outputs

- **Life:** master curve on $\tau_{eq}$, fitted on the R = −1 torsion failures (`Nf_expected`).
- **DP comparison:** supported. $\tau_{eq}$ is homogeneous of degree one in the load.
- **Fatigue-limit datasets:** the index uses `Taum1` as reference.
- No `_ext` variant.

## Verification and limitations

[`tests/methods/test_wang2018.py`](../../tests/methods/test_wang2018.py) checks:

- the estimated crack orientation of Section 5.1 (70°) for every 30° and 90° out-of-phase test of
  Table 5;
- that the three calibration limits of Eqs. (12)–(14) give $\tau_{eq} = \tau_{-1}$ exactly, with
  given or estimated $\sigma_0$, $\tau_0$;
- Eq. (20) against Eq. (15) with Eq. (19);
- the constant-$\tau_{\max}$ case of Eqs. (8)–(9), the scope error and the Eq. (17) material scope.

Not reproduced: the paper gives predicted lives only in figures (Figs. 11–12).

Limitations:

- tension–torsion only;
- constant amplitude only;
- the R = −1 tension limit is $\sqrt3\,\tau_{-1}$ by construction (von Mises form): the criterion
  does not use $\sigma_{-1}$ except through Eq. (19).
