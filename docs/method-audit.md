# Numerical method audit (7 October 2026)

This audit covers all 20 previously registered base methods, the shared plane evaluator, calibration, and reporting. It corrects identifiable implementation errors and separates usable damage-parameter implementations from incomplete proxies. Passing analytical tests establishes the tested numerical properties; it does not establish predictive accuracy for every material or reproduce every author's validation dataset.

The source catalogue is [bibliography.md](bibliography.md), backed by `src/multifat/models/references.py`. `get_models()` attaches a reference to each model, and new CSVs include `Method_reference` and `Implementation_revision`. No article PDFs are added to the repository.

## Retained methods

`tau_a`, `gamma_a`, and `epsilon_a` denote amplitudes; gamma is **engineering** shear strain (`gamma_ij = 2 epsilon_ij`). `sigma_max` is the plane-normal maximum. Except for FIN, existing tension-only normal-stress conventions remain as documented adaptations. The table distinguishes a checked numerical implementation from full independent source reproduction.

## Shared numerical conventions and limitations

- Workbook strains are constructed using linear isotropic elasticity. The workflow does not simulate cyclic plasticity or non-proportional hardening. GSE/GSA therefore have zero plastic contribution for these imported histories; external total-strain histories can have a plastic part.
- Non-proportional plane shear range follows each source where it prescribes one (2026-10-16): FS, MKBM, LI and LIU2021 define the range of the shear on the plane over the cycle, implemented as the longest chord of the shear path (2 x major semi-axis of the single-harmonic ellipse; `np_shear_rule = "longest_chord"`). Methods whose sources give no rule keep the repository's root-sum-square of the two semi-axes. BP/CAIM use the minimum-circumscribed-circle radius (major semi-axis). McDiarmid's out-of-phase plane association (plane of maximum principal-stress range) is not implemented. The RSS amplitude equals Papadopoulos' (2001) generalised shear amplitude, Eq. (10). Papadopoulos et al. (1997) restate Matake and McDiarmid with the circumscribed-circle amplitude instead: with that rule MATAKE and MCD reproduce their Table 1 columns to print precision (39/42 and 41/43 rows; `tests/methods/test_papadopoulos1997_matake_mcd.py`).
- Mean-shifted shear magnitude maxima use 720 time samples. GSE/GSA decompose the true harmonic tensors, not independently synthesized scalar extrema. The critical-plane search (2026-10-16) combines the 522-plane grid with analytical seed planes (Chiocca et al. 2023, 2024) and local refinement of the plane normal; the damage on the critical plane is the largest among converged planes tying the maximum selection metric. It reproduces the two-step and single-harmonic closed forms to 1e-9/1e-7 and is grid-independent to 1e-8; the global maximum is still found by search, not proven.
- BP/CAIM use 24 × 48 sphere quadrature; CAIM refines multiple grid maxima numerically. The MATLAB uses a different angular grid, so agreement is within discretization error, not bitwise equality.
- Missing R=0 strengths for BP/CAIM use the supplied 2026 article's estimates. They are maximum stresses, not amplitudes. These estimates and empirical strain-life properties are not measured values.
- Supplied material constants were calibrated with the old implementation. Recalibrate affected methods before drawing quantitative model rankings. Raw experimental workbooks and archived result CSVs are preserved.

## Damage-space error for every slope

`Error_log10_dp = log10(DP_e / DP)`, where `DP_e = DP_fit = A_surv * N_exp**b` and `DP = CP_value`. It is exported for calibration and assessment rows and included in statistics/plots for every slope. Positive values mean predicted allowable damage exceeds the evaluated point; negative values mean the point exceeds the curve. This sign convention is the reverse of the retained legacy `Error_ln_dp = ln(DP/DP_fit)`.

The default uses the median fitted curve (`STDNUM=0`, survival probability P_s = 0.5). `--survival-probability P_s` (`multifat-run`, `multifat-pipeline`; 0.5 <= P_s < 1, issue #16) shifts the curve down by z = Phi^-1(P_s) standard deviations of the ln DP residuals of the design tests. Under the assumption of normal residuals, at a given DP this is the life N_Ps = N_50 exp(-z STD), STD = STD_ln_DP/|b|, exceeded by a fraction P_s of specimens: 0.9 gives z = 1.28, 0.95 gives 1.64, 0.977 gives 2.0 (the former `STDNUM=2`), 0.99 gives 2.33. P_s is a survival probability, not a confidence level: the scatter is a point estimate from few tests and no tolerance factor is applied, and the regression is of ln DP on ln N rather than of ln N on ln DP (ASTM E739 style). Native life laws (MDC, MMCCM, MOREL, TVEIT2025) and fatigue-limit indices are not shifted. Every result CSV ends with `Survival_probability`; the sidecar records `master_curve_survival_probability` and `master_curve_stdnum`; `multifat-compare` refuses a results folder that mixes probabilities. For flat or rising curves the shift is a damage-space convention without a life interpretation, and no life is predicted (`DP_DIFF`). Nonpositive/nonfinite damage values yield NaN log ratios; they are not silently replaced by arbitrary positive values.

Life predictions are still suppressed when `b >= 0` or `k=-1/b > 15`. Exactly flat curves retain a finite constant `DP_e`. Constant-life calibration data cannot identify a slope and yield an invalid fit; the runner rejects it with an explanatory error. Parameter searches with no finite objective also fail explicitly instead of reporting an arbitrary bound as an optimum. Nonpositive evaluated DP values are retained with undefined log/life metrics instead of passing into life inversion. New columns do not change the meanings of `Error_ln` or `Error_ln_dp`.

## Constants fitted by the pipeline and native lives

`multifat-pipeline` fills only constants that a model requires and the workbook lacks. The fits are written to `<MATERIAL>_calibration.json`, never into the workbook. Basquin constants (sigma_f, b_fat, tau_f, b0) come from the workbook's fully reversed tension and torsion tests. A curve is used only with at least 5 tests, at least one decade of life and a slope beyond two standard errors; otherwise MDC and MMCCM are skipped with the reason. MMCCM's plastic terms (epsilon_f, c_fat, gamma_f, c0) are uniform-material-law estimates (steel form), because elastic HCF data cannot identify them. Torsion tests behind a fitted tau_f, b0 are written as design points (Flag = 0). `k_MGSE` comes from Zhu (2018) Eq. (12) on the Basquin fits whenever they are identifiable, also when no other selected model needs those fits (previously a run selecting MGSE_ZHU without MDC or MMCCM fitted `k_MGSE` by scatter minimisation instead, e.g. 1.239 rather than 0.988 on Al7075_T6); otherwise it falls back to scatter minimisation, recorded in `source`. Since November 2026 the same holds for the plane constants: the tension and torsion tests of the scatter objective behind a calibrated k_FI, k_FS, L_LI, k_MGSE or a_MSWT, and the Basquin tests behind a k_MGSE from Zhu (2018) Eq. (12), are design points of every model that adopts that constant (they were previously exported as Flag = 1, so their errors were not independent of the calibration). With `--constants`, a sidecar of this pipeline carries its design tests only when its `failure_fingerprint` matches the data; for an upstream record or a workbook constant the tests are unknown and remain Flag = 1 (`constants_source.design_rows` says so). Fitted constants are never passed to models that only read them optionally (GSA, LIU2021 keep their documented fallbacks). A constants file (`--constants`) may also supply the optional TVEIT2025 fatigue limits and numerical settings (`SUPPLIED_OPTIONAL` in `cli/pipeline.py`). MDC, MMCCM, MOREL and TVEIT2025 add `Nf_native` and `Error_log10_native = log10(Nf_exp/Nf_native)` from their own life laws.

## Result provenance

New CSVs carry `IMPLEMENTATION_REVISION` (currently `2026-11-11`, updated from `2026-10-07-audit1` whenever numerical outputs change). `Flag` is 0 for design points, 1 for predictions and 2 for run-outs: a run-out row has the recorded cycles in `Nf_exp` (a lower bound on life), `DP_fit` at that count (an upper bound on DP_e) and empty error columns, and is drawn with an arrow but never counted in statistics. The pipeline's `<MATERIAL>_calibration.json` records the workbook SHA-256, failure and run-out counts, git commit and dirty flag, package, Python and NumPy versions and the numerical settings. Comparison excludes disabled methods and pre-audit outputs by default. `--include-legacy` explicitly allows historical outputs from retained methods; it does not re-enable disabled methods. Do not mix corrected and old runs when ranking methods. See the README for regeneration commands.

## Verification

Run `python -m unittest discover -s tests -v`. Tests cover analytical plane values for all retained CP methods, mean-shear cancellation, Findley selection, plane ties, harmonic elastic/plastic splitting, shifted-ellipse maxima, disabled-name guards, bibliography coverage, flat/shallow fits, log base/sign, and export/statistics across both metric modes. BP/CAIM retain their separate limiting-case and angular-reference tests.

Full-text reproduction of the provisional methods listed above and independent validation against each publication's datasets remain outside the evidence established by these tests. They are explicitly retained with that limitation, instead of being labelled scientifically validated.
