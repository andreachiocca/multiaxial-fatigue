# Method taxonomy

Generated by `python tools/generate_method_docs.py` from the `approach`, `quantity`, `np_sensitivity`, `taxonomy_tags` and `taxonomy_basis` fields of [methods.json](catalogue/methods.json) (issue #61). The classes are mutually exclusive: a method belongs to the class of the step that reduces the multiaxial history to a scalar. Hybrids are classified by that final reduction, and the reason is in the note. `primary_family` is kept unchanged for backward compatibility.

## Axes

| Approach | Reduction to a scalar |
| --- | --- |
| `invariant` | Invariants or fixed-frame components of the whole cycle; no plane search (review family i). |
| `critical_plane` | A maximum over material planes (and possibly time) of a plane quantity (review family ii). |
| `integral` | An average or integral over all planes (review family iii). |
| `incremental` | Internal variables integrated along the history (review family iv). |
| `path` | A geometric measure of the load path is itself the damage scalar (review family v). |

- **Quantity**: `stress`, `strain` or `energy` (stress times strain).
- **Non-proportionality**: `explicit` when a term or a calibrated curve acts only on non-proportional loading; `emergent` when the response to it follows from the reduction alone.
- **Tags**: `mesoscopic`, `native_life`, `probabilistic`, `nonlocal_gradient`, `damage_accumulation`, `application_specific`. Mesoscopic is a tag, not a class: Dang Van, Papadopoulos (2001) and Morel take a maximum over planes.
- **Basis**: `code` (registered, read from the module), `source` (from the reviewed original paper), `catalogue_principle` (from the catalogue entry, source not reviewed), `partial` (approach not determinable from the entry), `undetermined`.
