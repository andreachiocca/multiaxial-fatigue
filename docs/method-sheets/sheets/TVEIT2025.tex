\methodhead{TVEIT2025}{Tveit, Ottosen, Erduran and Reyes (2025): continuum fatigue model with a moving endurance surface}\label{sheet:TVEIT2025}

\block{Source} Tveit, N.~S.~Ottosen, E.~Erduran, A.~Reyes, \emph{Mech.\ Mater.}\ 209 (2025) 105452
\cite{tveit2025}, Eqs.~(1)--(12), pp.~2--4, and Eq.~(24), p.~5; Figs.~5--6, Table~1. This sheet is
written from the method guide and the module docstring only (the model is maintained by another
contributor).

\block{Idea and scope} An incremental continuum damage model integrated cycle by cycle. A back-stress
tensor $\boldsymbol\alpha$ and a scalar damage $D$ evolve whenever the stress leaves a moving endurance
surface ($\beta>0$) and moves outward ($d\beta>0$). Failure (crack initiation) occurs at $D=1$; a path
that the moved surface ends up enclosing accumulates no more damage and has an infinite life. The
deviatoric measure carries a $J_3$ term, so arbitrary ratios $\tm/\fm$ are represented.

\block{Formulation} The moving endurance surface (Eqs.~1--5, 24):
\begin{gather}
\beta = \frac{\sigma_e + A\,I_1 - \fm}{\fm} , \label{eq:tveit-beta}\\
\sigma_e = \sqrt{3J_2} - \lambda\Bigl(1 - \frac{27}{4}\frac{J_3^2}{J_2^3}\Bigr) , \label{eq:tveit-se}\\
\lambda = \sqrt3\,\tm - \fm , \label{eq:tveit-lambda}
\end{gather}
with $J_2$, $J_3$ of $\mathbf s-\boldsymbol\alpha$. While $\beta>0$ and $d\beta>0$ (Eq.~8), the
evolution laws are (Eqs.~6, 7, 9--12)
\begin{gather}
d\boldsymbol\alpha = C\,(\mathbf s-\boldsymbol\alpha)\,d\beta , \qquad
dD = K\,e^{L\beta}\,d\beta , \label{eq:tveit-evol}\\
d\beta = \frac{\mathbf G:d\mathbf s + A\,dI_1}{\fm + C\,\mathbf G:(\mathbf s-\boldsymbol\alpha)} , \qquad
\mathbf G = \frac{\partial\sigma_e}{\partial\mathbf s} . \label{eq:tveit-dbeta}
\end{gather}
Starting from $\boldsymbol\alpha=\mathbf 0$ and $D=0$, the number of cycles to $D=1$ is the life.

\block{Constants and calibration} Paper: $\fm$, $\tm$ from fully reversed tests; $A$ from a fatigue
limit at a tensile mean stress (Eqs.~22--23), else an estimate (FKM, Eq.~26); $C$, $K$, $L$ by
Eq.~(25) on three failures of the axial $R=-1$ S--N curve, after Ottosen et al.\ \cite{ottosen2008}.
Repository: without supplied constants, \texttt{multifat-pipeline} calibrates \texttt{A\_TVEIT},
\texttt{C\_TVEIT}, \texttt{K\_TVEIT} and \texttt{L\_TVEIT} by this procedure
(\texttt{eval/tveit2025\_calibration.py}). The limits are the workbook Summary values; $A$ comes, in
this order, from a mean-stress fatigue limit, a hand-entered value, FKM for a declared material type,
or Goodman, $A=\fm/S_u$; the three points are the lowest and highest stress levels above $\fm$ and the
level closest to their geometric mean (the median replicate at each), and they become design rows.
Uniaxial data do not identify $C$, so Eq.~(25) is profiled over $C$ (8 values per decade) and the grid
point of lowest error is kept whose closed form has a unique root and whose time-integrated life agrees
with it within 0.1 decade. Each calibration reports a confidence (high, medium, low) from named
findings. A \texttt{--constants} file takes precedence; it may also set \texttt{Sigm1\_TVEIT} and
\texttt{Taum1\_TVEIT}, which replace the workbook \texttt{Sigm1}, \texttt{Taum1} for this model only,
since the constants hold only with the $\fm$, $\tm$ they were calibrated with. The calibration runs only
when none of the model's parameters is entered by hand (constants file or Summary); with only some of
them entered, the model is skipped with the reason.

\block{Life} Native life law: the integration of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:tveit-beta}--\eqref{eq:tveit-dbeta} to
$D=1$ (\texttt{life\_prediction = "native"}); $N_f=\infty$ when the path is enclosed by the moved
surface. Only native lives are compared with experiments; the damage-parameter comparison is disabled
(\texttt{dp\_comparison = False}).

\block{Repository choices}
\begin{itemize}
\item The cycle is sampled at 1001 points of one closed period and repeated as blocks (one block = one
  cycle), with explicit increments between consecutive points.
\item Blocks are repeated until: \emph{failure}, $D\ge1$ inside a block, the life interpolated linearly
  in the crossing increment; \emph{shakedown}, a block (from the second on) without damage increment,
  infinite life; \emph{cyclic steady state}, from block 400 on, when the back-stress has no net drift
  over the last 200 blocks (distance to its position 200 blocks earlier at most 0.01 times its path
  length) and the mean damage per block agrees within 0.1\,\% with the 200 blocks before, unless the
  trend of the peak of $\beta$ over the block reaches zero before the extrapolated failure; the life is
  then $N=n+(1-D)/\overline{dD}$; after 60\,000 blocks the life is extrapolated in the same way, with a
  warning.
\item These settings are optional constants (\texttt{npts\_TVEIT}, \texttt{min\_blocks\_TVEIT},
  \texttt{max\_blocks\_TVEIT}, \texttt{tol\_alpha\_TVEIT}, \texttt{tol\_damage\_TVEIT}). With 1001 points
  per cycle the life is about 0.02 decade shorter than converged (Table~1 NiCrMo steel).
\item $\mathbf G$ uses the deviatoric projection $\mathbf s\mathbf s-\tfrac23J_2\mathbf I$ of
  $\partial J_3/\partial\mathbf s$; Eq.~(10) prints a different isotropic part, which has no effect
  because $\mathbf G$ is contracted only with deviatoric tensors.
\item The reported value is the fatigue index $\max_t(\sigma_e+A\,I_1)/\fm$ at $\boldsymbol\alpha=\mathbf 0$
  ($\beta+1$ of the virgin material); the model's prediction is the native life.
\end{itemize}

\block{Worked example} Stage~1 constants for 42CrMo4 Q\&T (constants file
\texttt{Calibrations/42CrMo4\_Santus/TVEIT2025\_stage1.json}): $\fm=433$, $\tm=344\MPa$, $A=0.245625$,
$C=0.0040672184319305$, $K=1.91047799401683\times10^{-5}$, $L=10.1293188952565$. Bending
$\sigma_a=660\MPa$ and torsion $\tau_a=340\MPa$ at $90^\circ$: index $1.8986$, $N_f=263$ cycles.

\block{Verification} The model S--N curves of Figs.~5--6 (Table~1 constants) are reproduced within 1\,\%
in stress; also checked: the Eq.~(24) torsional limit, infinite life inside the endurance surface and
after shakedown under mean shear, and the constants-file overrides.
