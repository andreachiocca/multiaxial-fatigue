% registry: SWT
\methodhead{SWT}{Smith, Watson and Topper (1970): stress--strain function, critical-plane extension}\label{sheet:SWT}

\block{Source} K.~N.~Smith, P.~Watson, T.~H.~Topper, \emph{J.\ Mater.} 5(4) (1970) 767--778
\cite{smith1970}, p.~769. The paper has no DOI. The critical-plane extension is not in the 1970
paper; the repository's guide attributes it to later work (Socie 1987; Socie and Marquis 2000).

\block{Idea and scope} Damage is driven by the normal strain amplitude multiplied by the largest
tensile stress that opens the crack. The product accounts for the mean stress without a separate
correction, and a fully compressive cycle causes no damage. Proposed for uniaxial mean-stress
effects; in its critical-plane form, for materials that fail mainly by tensile (mode~I) cracking.

\block{Formulation} The paper defines the damage function as $\sigma_{\max}\varepsilon_a$ and uses
it as the equivalent stress (p.~769)
\begin{equation}
\sqrt{\sigma_{\max}\,\varepsilon_a\,E} = \text{constant at a given life},
\qquad \sigma_{\max}>0 .
\label{eq:swt-main}
\end{equation}
A cycle with $\sigma_{\max}\le0$ causes no damage. Combined with the Basquin and Manson--Coffin
curves it gives the common life relation
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\max}\,\varepsilon_a = \frac{\sigma_f'^2}{E}(2N_f)^{2b} + \sigma_f'\,\varepsilon_f'\,(2N_f)^{b+c} .
\label{eq:swt-life}
\end{equation}
The critical-plane extension evaluates, on each plane,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{DP} = \sigma_{n,\max}\,\varepsilon_{n,a},\qquad
\varepsilon_{n,a} = \tfrac12\lvert\Delta\varepsilon_n\rvert,\qquad
\sigma_{n,\max} = \max(\sigma_{n,m}+\sigma_{n,a},\,0) .
\label{eq:swt-plane}
\end{equation}
\emph{Critical plane}: the plane of largest normal strain range $\Delta\varepsilon_n$; ties go to
the larger DP (Section~\ref{sec:planes}). \texttt{SWT\_ext} is the largest DP over all planes.
\emph{Shear amplitude}: the parameter uses no shear quantity, so the plane shear rule
(\texttt{np\_shear\_rule}, default \texttt{rss}) and the global selection of
\texttt{docs/shear-amplitude.md} do not change its value.

\block{Constants and calibration} None. In the original form the constants of the life relation
are $\sigma_f'$, $b$, $\varepsilon_f'$, $c$ of the uniaxial strain--life curve. The repository fits
only the master curve. With elastic strains, fully reversed tension gives $\mathrm{DP}=\sigma_a^2/E$
and fully reversed torsion (plane at $45^\circ$) $\mathrm{DP}=(1+\nu)\tau_a^2/E$, so equal damage at
the two limits implies $\tm/\fm = 1/\sqrt{1+\nu}\approx0.88$ for $\nu=0.3$.

\block{Life} The paper uses Eq.~\eqref{eq:swt-life}. The repository uses the master curve on DP,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mastercurve}, fitted on the uniaxial $R=-1$ design set.

\block{Repository choices}
\begin{itemize}
\item DP is the product $\sigma_{\max}\varepsilon_a$, not $\sqrt{\sigma_{\max}\varepsilon_a E}$. The
  transform is monotone: master-curve lives are unchanged, but the fitted exponent and the
  log-damage errors are doubled.
\item $\sigma_{n,\max}$ is clipped at zero, so a fully compressive cycle gives $\mathrm{DP}=0$.
\item The critical-plane extension is not in the 1970 paper.
\item Strains are linear elastic (Section~\ref{sec:notation}).
\item Life from the shared master curve instead of Eq.~\eqref{eq:swt-life}.
\end{itemize}

\block{Worked example} Synthetic material, $E=200\,000\MPa$, $\nu=0.3$, zero-mean unless stated:
tension $\sigma_a=300\MPa$ gives $\mathrm{DP}=0.4500\MPa$ ($=\sigma_a^2/E$); torsion
$\tau_a=200\MPa$ gives $0.2600\MPa$ ($=(1+\nu)\tau_a^2/E$); tension $\sigma_a=300\MPa$ with a mean of
$150\MPa$ gives $0.6750\MPa$ ($=(150+300)\cdot300/E$); a cycle of mean $-400\MPa$ and amplitude
$100\MPa$ gives $0.0$. The limit ratio of equal DP is $1/\sqrt{1+\nu}=0.877$.

\block{Verification} \texttt{tests/methods/test\_swt.py}: the uniaxial product with tensile, zero
and compressive means, and zero damage for a fully compressive cycle. Not reproduced: the 1970
experimental data or any multiaxial dataset.
