% registry: GAM
\methodhead{GAM}{Gon\c{c}alves, Ara\'ujo and Mamiya (2005): stress-based criterion for hard metals}\label{sheet:GAM}

\block{Source} C.~A.~Gon\c{c}alves, J.~A.~Ara\'ujo, E.~N.~Mamiya, \emph{Int.\ J.\ Fatigue} 27 (2005)
177--187 \cite{goncalves2005}, Eqs.~(11)--(14), Appendix~A (Eqs.~A.1--A.5, A.17); Tables~1--4.

\block{Idea and scope} An endurance criterion of the effective-stress type with two measures of the
history. The shear solicitation is the size of the rectangular prism that encloses the deviatoric
path; it is larger for a non-proportional path than for a proportional one of equal
$\sqrt{J_{2,a}}$. The normal solicitation is the \emph{maximum principal stress} over the cycle, in
place of the hydrostatic stress: the embryo crack normal to the largest principal stress governs the
opening effect. Proposed for high-cycle fatigue limits of hard metals,
$1.3\le\fm/\tm<\sqrt3$, under in-phase and out-of-phase sinusoidal loading of one frequency, with
mean stresses.

\block{Formulation} The endurance condition (Eq.~12) is
\begin{equation}
\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{5} a_i^2} + \kappa\,\sigma_{p\max} \le \lambda ,
\label{eq:gam-main}
\end{equation}
with, writing $\mathbf S(t)=\sum_i s_i(t)\mathbf N_i$ (Eqs.~A.1--A.3),
\begin{gather}
a_i = \tfrac12\Bigl(\max_t s_i(t) - \min_t s_i(t)\Bigr) \quad\text{(A.17)}, \label{eq:gam-ai}\\
\sigma_{p\max} = \max_t \max_i \sigma_{p,i}(t) \quad\text{(11)}. \label{eq:gam-sp}
\end{gather}
Proposition~1 (Eq.~A.5): for a path enclosed by an ellipsoid with semi-axes $\lambda_i$,
$\sum_i a_i^2=\sum_i\lambda_i^2$ in \emph{any} orthonormal basis, which avoids the construction of the
minimum enclosing ellipsoid (Eqs.~8--9). The constants follow from the fully reversed bending and
torsion limits (Eq.~13):
\begin{equation}
\kappa = \frac{\sqrt2}{\fm-\tm}\Bigl(\tm - \frac{\fm}{\sqrt3}\Bigr) , \qquad
\lambda = \sqrt2\,\frac{\tm\,\fm}{\fm-\tm}\Bigl(1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt3}\Bigr) .
\label{eq:gam-const}
\end{equation}
They place both limits on the threshold: in bending $\sqrt{\sum a_i^2}=\sqrt{2/3}\,\fm$ and
$\sigma_{p\max}=\fm$; in torsion $\sqrt{\sum a_i^2}=\sqrt2\,\tm$ and $\sigma_{p\max}=\tm$. The error
index is $I=100\,(\mathrm{LHS}-\lambda)/\lambda$ (Eq.~14), positive on the conservative side. The
repository returns $\mathrm{DP}=\bigl(\sqrt{\sum a_i^2}+\kappa\,\sigma_{p\max}\bigr)/\lambda$, so
$I=100\,(\mathrm{DP}-1)$.

\block{Constants and calibration} Paper and repository: $\kappa$ and $\lambda$ in closed form from
$\fm$ and $\tm$ (Eq.~13), with the $R=-1$ limits of the workbook Summary. Nothing is fitted except the
master-curve constants $A$, $b$ on the uniaxial $R=-1$ design tests. A material whose limits are
missing or flagged is skipped.

\block{Life} No native life law. The index is the damage parameter of the shared master curve
(Section~\ref{sec:mastercurve}); this extends an endurance criterion beyond its source. The index is
homogeneous of degree~1 in the load.

\block{Repository choices}
\begin{itemize}
\item For one harmonic, $s_i(t)=m_i+p_i\sin\omega t+q_i\cos\omega t$, so $a_i^2=p_i^2+q_i^2$ and,
  the basis being orthonormal, $\sqrt{\sum a_i^2}=\sqrt{\lVert\dev\Ss\rVert^2+\lVert\dev\Sc\rVert^2}$
  (Frobenius norms), exact and basis-free.
\item Eq.~\eqref{eq:gam-sp} as written: $\sigma_{p\max}$ is the largest eigenvalue of $\Tsig(t)$
  maximised over the cycle (720 time samples, then a golden-section refinement). The paper's tables
  used a phase-free peak instead (see Verification).
\item $\fm\le\tm$ is rejected ($\kappa$, $\lambda$ undefined). Outside $1.3\le\fm/\tm<\sqrt3$ the
  criterion is evaluated with a warning; for $\fm/\tm\ge\sqrt3$, $\kappa\le0$.
\item Finite life through the master curve, a repository extension. No \texttt{\_ext} variant.
\end{itemize}

\block{Worked example} Hard steel of Nishihara and Kawamoto (paper's Table~1), $\fm=313.9$,
$\tm=196.2\MPa$: $\kappa=0.1799$, $\lambda=312.76\MPa$. Row 1-1, in phase ($\sigma_a=138.1$,
$\tau_a=167.1\MPa$): $I=-1.91\,\%$. Row 1-4, $90^\circ$ ($\sigma_a=150.2$, $\tau_a=181.7\MPa$): with
Eq.~(11), $\sigma_{p\max}=199.5\MPa$ and $I=+2.51\,\%$; with the paper's in-phase peak,
$\sigma_{p\max}=271.7\MPa$ and the printed $I=+6.66\,\%$.

\block{Verification} Tables~1--4, column $I^d$ (41 fatigue limits, four steels). All 14 in-phase rows
are reproduced to 0.02 points by Eq.~(12) as written. All 41 rows are reproduced to 0.02 points if
$\sigma_{p\max}$ is replaced by the phase-free peak $s/2+\sqrt{s^2/4+x^2}$, $s=\sigma_a+\sigma_m$,
$x=\tau_a+\tau_m$. With Eq.~(11) the 27 out-of-phase rows are 0.3 to 13.4 points lower; they are
strict xfails, recording a calculation inconsistency of the tables, not of the criterion. Also
checked: Eq.~(13) puts both limits on the threshold; the closed-form $\sqrt{\sum a_i^2}$ equals the
half-ranges of Eq.~(A.17) in the basis of Eq.~(A.3) for a random out-of-phase path; the rejection of
$\fm\le\tm$ and the hard-metal warning.
