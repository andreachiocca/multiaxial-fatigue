\methodhead{WANG2022}{Wang, Sun, Liu, Liu and Shang (2022): equivalent shear stress on the maximum shear amplitude plane}\label{sheet:WANG2022}

\block{Source} Y.~Wang, G.-Q.~Sun, J.~Liu, X.~Liu, D.~Shang, \emph{Int.\ J.\ Damage Mech.} 31 (2022)
1165--1186 \cite{wang2022}, Eqs.~(10), (15), (18)--(24); Tables~3--7.

\block{Idea and scope} On the plane of maximum shear stress amplitude $C_a$, the shear amplitude and a
weighted normal stress amplitude are combined into an equivalent shear stress $t_{eq,a}$; the life
then follows from the material's torsional S--N curve. The weight contains a maximum equivalent normal
stress of SWT form, $\sqrt{N_{\max}N_a}$, meant to account for mean stress. Proposed for high-cycle
fatigue under proportional and out-of-phase tension (or bending) and torsion: aluminium alloys,
30CrMnSiA and SM45C steels. The repository covers fully reversed loading only (see Repository
choices).

\block{Formulation} On the plane of maximum $C_a$ (Eqs.~18--19),
\begin{gather}
t_{eq,a} = \sqrt{C_a^2 + k\Bigl(1-\frac{N^*_{\max}}{2\sigma_y}\Bigr)^2 N_a^2} \le \lambda = \tm ,
\qquad N^*_{\max}=\sqrt{N_{\max}N_a},\quad N_{\max}=N_a+N_m , \label{eq:w22}\\
k = \frac{4\tm^2-\fm^2}{\fm^2\bigl(1-\fm/(4\sigma_y)\bigr)^2} . \label{eq:w22-k}
\end{gather}
$k$ makes both fully reversed limits satisfy the criterion; $k>0$ requires $\tm/\fm>0.5$ (Eq.~21).
\emph{Critical plane}: the plane of maximum $C_a$. \emph{Ties} of the maximum $C_a$ go to the larger
$t_{eq,a}$. \emph{Shear amplitude}: $C_a$ is half the longest chord of the shear path
(\texttt{longest\_chord}, MCC), as Eq.~(10) defines it. The model is registered at case level and its
amplitude is fixed by its definition: the global selection of \texttt{docs/shear-amplitude.md} leaves
it unchanged.

\block{Constants and calibration} Paper: $k$ from $\fm$, $\tm$ and $\sigma_y$ (Eq.~19); the life
curve is the material's torsional S--N curve, fitted on torsion tests. Repository: the same $k$ from
workbook \texttt{Sigm1}, \texttt{Taum1}, \texttt{Sy}. The master curve $AN_f^b$ is fitted on the
workbook's fully reversed pure torsion failures (\texttt{design\_loading = "torsion"}), which become
design rows; tension and multiaxial tests are predictions. A workbook without such torsion failures
skips the model.

\block{Life} Paper (Eqs.~22--24): $t_{eq,a}$ equals the torsional S--N curve, either
$\tau_f'(2N_f)^{b_1}$ (Basquin) or $\tm/(1-\kappa N_f^{b_2})$ (three-parameter form). Repository: the
master curve $t_{eq,a}=AN_f^b$ on the torsion design set (Section~\ref{sec:mastercurve}).

\block{Repository choices}
\begin{itemize}
\item Mean stress (maintainer decision). With Eq.~\eqref{eq:w22} as printed, a tensile mean normal
  stress raises $N^*_{\max}$ and so lowers $t_{eq,a}$, i.e.\ predicts a longer life, contrary to the
  paper's text and to the experimental evidence. The printed form is kept because it reproduces every
  predicted life of Tables~4--5 (no mean stress); a ``$+$'' sign reproduces none, and the SM45C
  mean-stress lives of Table~7 are reproduced by neither. A load that puts a mean normal stress on the
  critical plane is out of scope (\texttt{LoadingScopeError}): the critical plane is found first, and
  $|N_m|$ there is checked.
\item $C_a$ by the longest chord (Eq.~10); ties of the maximum $C_a$ towards the larger $t_{eq,a}$.
\item Life: a single power law fitted on the pure torsion $R=-1$ failures replaces the
  three-parameter form of Eq.~(23).
\item Registered as a case-level model (the scope check needs the critical plane), so there is no
  \texttt{\_ext} variant.
\end{itemize}
