% registry: FS
\methodhead{FS}{Fatemi and Socie (1988): critical plane approach including out-of-phase loading}\label{sheet:FS}

\block{Source} A.~Fatemi, D.~F.~Socie, \emph{Fatigue Fract.\ Eng.\ Mater.\ Struct.} 11 (1988)
149--165 \cite{fatemi1988}, Eqs.~(8)--(10).

\block{Idea and scope} Strain-based critical-plane criterion. Cracks nucleate and grow on the
planes of maximum shear strain; a tensile normal stress on those planes opens the crack faces,
reduces the friction between them and accelerates growth. Proposed for low- and high-cycle fatigue
of ductile metals that fail by shear cracking, including out-of-phase loading with additional
(non-proportional) hardening.

\block{Formulation} On the plane of maximum shear strain amplitude (Eq.~8),
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Delta\gamma_{\max}}{2}\left(1+n\,\frac{\sigma_n^{\max}}{\sigma_y}\right)=\text{constant} .
\label{eq:fs-main}
\end{equation}
With the uniaxial strain--life curve the paper obtains (Eq.~9)
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Delta\gamma_{\max}}{2}\left(1+n\frac{\sigma_n^{\max}}{\sigma_y}\right)
=(1+\nu_e)\frac{\sigma_f'}{E}(2N_f)^b+\frac n2(1+\nu_e)\frac{\sigma_f'^2}{E\,\sigma_y}(2N_f)^{2b}
+(1+\nu_p)\,\varepsilon_f'(2N_f)^c+\frac n2(1+\nu_p)\frac{\varepsilon_f'\sigma_f'}{\sigma_y}(2N_f)^{b+c} ,
\label{eq:fs-life}
\end{equation}
whose coefficients become those of Eq.~(10) with $n=0.6$, $\nu_e=0.29$, $\nu_p=0.5$. The repository
evaluates on every candidate plane
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{DP}=\gamma_a\left(1+k_\mathrm{FS}\,\frac{\sigma_{n,\max}}{S_y}\right),\qquad
\sigma_{n,\max}=\max(\sigma_{n,m}+\sigma_{n,a},\,0),
\label{eq:fs-dp}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_a$ is the norm of the range of the tensorial shear strain on the plane, which equals
the engineering shear amplitude ($\gamma=2\varepsilon$), so no factor 2 is applied. \texttt{FS}
takes the plane of largest $\gamma_a$ (the source's plane rule; among tied planes the larger DP);
\texttt{FS\_ext} takes the largest DP over all planes (Section~\ref{sec:planes}).

\block{Constants and calibration} In the paper, $n$ is chosen so that uniaxial and torsional data
collapse on the FS parameter, i.e.\ by equating Eq.~\eqref{eq:fs-life} with the torsional
strain--life curve at the lives of interest. In the repository $k_\mathrm{FS}$ comes from the
workbook, from \texttt{--constants}, or from the pipeline: $k_\mathrm{FS}\in[0,3]$ minimises the
standard deviation of $\ln N_f$ of the $R=-1$ tension and torsion failures about one master curve
(one constant for all lives). These failures become design rows. The FS plane does not depend on
$k_\mathrm{FS}$, because it is chosen by $\gamma_a$. On fatigue-limit datasets $k_\mathrm{FS}$
equalises the DP of the reversed tension and torsion limits; with elastic strains,
$k_\mathrm{FS}=2\,(2\tm/\fm-1)\,S_y/\fm$.

\block{Life} The paper uses Eq.~\eqref{eq:fs-life}. The repository uses the master curve on DP
(Section~\ref{sec:mastercurve}) instead.

\block{Repository choices}
\begin{itemize}
\item $\sigma_{n,\max}<0$ is clipped to 0, so compression never lowers DP.
\item The workbook monotonic yield strength $S_y$ replaces the cyclic yield strength.
\item Strains are linear-elastic from $E$ and $\nu$; cyclic plasticity and non-proportional
  hardening, a key motivation of the original model, are not modelled. In the high-cycle regime of
  the supplied datasets $\gamma_a=\tau_a/G$.
\item Out-of-phase loading: $\gamma_a$ is half the longest chord of the shear-strain ellipse
  (\texttt{np\_shear\_rule = "longest\_chord"}), the maximum shear range on the plane over the
  cycle, as the source defines it.
\item $k_\mathrm{FS}$ is calibrated as above and assumed independent of life.
\item \texttt{FS\_ext} is a repository variant. Closed forms for this maximum under proportional
  elastic loading exist (Chiocca et al., 2024); the repository uses the numerical plane search.
\end{itemize}

\block{Worked example} Synthetic material (not experimental data): $E=200$\,GPa, $\nu=0.3$,
$S_y=400$, $\fm=300$, $\tm=200\MPa$. Equal DP at the two limits gives $k_\mathrm{FS}=0.8889$. Then
FS at the limits: tension 0.002600, torsion 0.002600. \texttt{FS\_ext} in tension: 0.002676 on the
plane at $38.5^\circ$ from the axis, where
$\mathrm{DP}(\theta)=(1+\nu)\frac{\sigma_a}{E}\sin2\theta\,(1+k_\mathrm{FS}\,\sigma_a\cos^2\theta/S_y)$
is largest. Tension with $\sigma_m=100$, $\sigma_a=250\MPa$: FS $=0.002257$, equal to the closed form
$(1+\nu)\frac{\sigma_a}{E}\bigl(1+k_\mathrm{FS}\frac{\sigma_m+\sigma_a}{2S_y}\bigr)$.

\block{Verification} Checked: the Eq.~(10) coefficients from Eq.~\eqref{eq:fs-life}; uniaxial
tension with mean stress (45$^\circ$ plane, $\gamma_a=(1+\nu)\varepsilon_a$,
$\sigma_n^{\max}=\sigma_{\max}/2$); engineering shear in pure torsion; the longest-chord shear range
out of phase. Not reproduced: the experimental data of the paper.
